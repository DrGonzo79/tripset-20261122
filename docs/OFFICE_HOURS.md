# Office Hours diagnostic

## Specific customer
Frequent-traveling strength clients already following a coach-authored program. The economic buyer for the first test is the traveler; the coach is the trust gate and later channel partner.

## Painful status quo
On arrival, equipment differs from expectations. The traveler guesses, skips the workout, or interrupts the coach. The coach recreates sessions manually in chat, often after hours.

## Desperate use case
“My prescribed heavy Tuesday is tonight, the hotel gym is sparse, and I need a safe equivalent that still reflects what my coach programmed.” This is time-bounded, concrete, and testable.

## Narrowest paid wedge
A **$29 one-trip adaptation**: confirm hotel inventory, import one week of prescribed sessions, and receive rule-governed substitutes plus coach-review escalations. Do not start with an all-purpose workout generator or a coach platform.

## Observation and validation
Recruit 10 traveling lifters through coach communities. Watch each inventory-confirmation and substitution-review session. Success: 7 complete without help, 5 say it avoided a coach message, and 3 prepay $29 for a real upcoming trip. Track review rate, time-to-session, rejected substitutions and coach overrides. Kill or narrow if fewer than 2 prepay after 10 observed sessions.

## Future fit
Only after paid trip demand: saved hotel inventories, coach rule templates, and roster plans. A useful moat could emerge from coach-approved equipment/movement mappings and override outcomes, not generic AI generation.
